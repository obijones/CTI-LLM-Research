**Executive Threat Intelligence Report – Ransomware Risk**

**1. Executive Summary**

LockBit 3.0 ransomware affiliates are actively targeting organizations across multiple critical infrastructure sectors, posing a significant and immediate risk to our operations. This threat actor’s observed tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) indicate a capability to disrupt our financial services operations, potentially leading to substantial financial losses and reputational damage. We must take immediate action to mitigate this risk.

**2. Potential Business Impact**

A successful LockBit 3.0 attack could result in significant operational disruption, potentially halting critical financial transactions and impacting our ability to serve customers. The financial impact could range from direct ransom payments – which are never advised – to substantial recovery costs, including system restoration, data recovery, and legal fees. Furthermore, a breach could trigger regulatory scrutiny and potential fines related to data protection and operational resilience, damaging our reputation and eroding customer trust.

**3. Affected Systems / Scope**

This threat actor’s activity is focused on organizations utilizing Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway software, impacting systems that support core financial processes. Specifically, compromised systems could disrupt access to critical data, halt payment processing, and impede our ability to manage customer accounts. This exposure affects all critical infrastructure organizations and network defenders using Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway software.

**4. Recommended Actions**

*   **Immediately isolate** potentially affected hosts – This action directly addresses the recommended incident response step of quarantining or taking offline potentially affected hosts, minimizing the potential for further compromise and data exfiltration.
*   **Reimage compromised hosts** – Reimaging compromised hosts is a key step in eradicating the threat, aligning with the recommended action of re-imaging compromised hosts to eliminate persistent malware and restore systems to a secure state.
*   **Create new account credentials** – Establishing new account credentials following a suspected compromise is a critical step in preventing further unauthorized access, mirroring the recommendation to create new account credentials after a potential compromise is detected.
*   **Report the compromise** – Reporting the compromise to the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) and CISA via the agency’s Incident Reporting System is essential for sharing information and receiving support, aligning with the recommended reporting procedures outlined in the source report.
*   **Assess Citrix software and your systems** – Organizations are encouraged to assess Citrix software and your systems for evidence of compromise, and to hunt for malicious activity, ensuring a thorough evaluation of our systems for potential vulnerabilities.
