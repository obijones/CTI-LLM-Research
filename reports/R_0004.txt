**BOTTOM LINE: Immediately initiate a review of all third-party vendor relationships to mitigate the risk of data breaches and operational disruptions.**

**1. Executive Summary**

A significant ransomware attack targeting Change Healthcare has created a substantial risk to the broader healthcare ecosystem. The attack, perpetrated by a ransomware affiliate, resulted in the theft of protected health information potentially impacting up to one in three Americans. This poses a direct threat to patient safety, operational continuity, and the financial stability of healthcare providers.

**2. Potential Business Impact**

This incident presents a severe risk to our organization’s operations and reputation. The potential for widespread disruption to healthcare services, including medication delivery and patient care, could lead to significant financial losses due to lost productivity and potential legal liabilities. Furthermore, the exposure of sensitive patient data creates a substantial risk of reputational damage and potential regulatory scrutiny from bodies like the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).

**3. Affected Systems / Scope**

The ransomware attack has compromised Change Healthcare’s systems, which are used by numerous healthcare providers across the United States. This exposes a wide range of operational functions, including patient data management, claims processing, and supply chain logistics. The scale of the breach – potentially affecting data for one in three Americans – represents a systemic risk to the entire healthcare industry.

**4. Recommended Actions**

*   **Prioritize Vendor Risk Assessment:** Immediately conduct a comprehensive review of all third-party vendor relationships, focusing on those with access to sensitive patient data, to identify potential vulnerabilities and weaknesses.
*   **Enhance Cybersecurity Spending:** Increase investment in cybersecurity measures, particularly those focused on preventing ransomware attacks and data breaches, given the demonstrated need within the healthcare sector.
*   **Strengthen Incident Response Planning:** Develop and implement robust incident response plans to rapidly contain and mitigate the impact of future cyberattacks, mirroring the urgent need identified by CMS Principal Deputy Administrator Jonathan Blum.
*   **Monitor Regulatory Developments:** Closely track regulatory developments and guidance from agencies like the HHS and the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) regarding data breach notification and cybersecurity standards.
*   **Assess Board Oversight:** Evaluate the governance and oversight of cybersecurity practices within organizations, particularly those with significant exposure to cyber risk, to ensure appropriate levels of scrutiny and accountability.
