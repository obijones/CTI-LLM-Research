**Executive Threat Intelligence Report – Cisco IOS XE WebUI Privesc Vulnerabilities**

**To:** Senior Business Leadership
**From:** [Your Name/Cybersecurity Analysis Team]
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Urgent: Active Exploitation of Cisco IOS XE WebUI Vulnerabilities – Immediate Mitigation Required

**Executive Summary:**

We’ve identified an active and escalating threat campaign targeting Cisco IOS XE devices utilizing a privilege escalation vulnerability (CVE-2023-20198 and CVE-2023-20273).  The threat actor is actively exploiting these vulnerabilities, deploying a malicious web shell (“BadCandy”) to gain unauthorized access and control of affected systems. This activity began with the publication of Proof-of-Concept (POC) code and has rapidly intensified, indicating a significant and ongoing risk.

**Key Findings:**

* **Active Exploitation:**  The threat actor is actively exploiting these vulnerabilities, as evidenced by a dramatic increase in exploitation attempts following the release of the POCs.
* **Malicious Web Shell (“BadCandy”):** The attacker is deploying a Lua-based web shell, “BadCandy,” to establish persistent access. This shell is used for reconnaissance, data exfiltration, and potentially further malicious activity.
* **Tactics Observed:** We’ve observed the following tactics employed by the attacker:
    * **Initial Reconnaissance:** Gathering information about the device and conducting preliminary reconnaissance using commands like `clear logging`, `no username cisco_support`, and attempts to create and delete user accounts.
    * **Evidence Clearing:** The attacker is actively clearing logs and removing users to conceal their activity, specifically targeting the `cisco_tac_admin` account.
    * **Implant Deployment:**  The actor expanded their operation to include deploying the “BadCandy” web shell, suggesting a move towards persistent access.
* **Timeline of Activity:** The initial suspicious activity was detected as early as September 18th, 2023, with a significant cluster of activity observed in October.
* **Alignment with Guidance:** Cisco’s response is consistent with guidance from the U.S. government (Binding Operational Directive 23-02) and reinforces best practices for mitigating risk from internet-exposed management interfaces.

**Risk Assessment:**

* **Severity:** High – This vulnerability allows for complete system compromise.
* **Impact:** Potential data breach, disruption of network services, and potential for further malicious activity (e.g., ransomware deployment, lateral movement).
* **Current Status:**  The threat actor is actively exploiting these vulnerabilities.

**Recommendations – Immediate Action Required:**

1. **Disable HTTP/S Server:** Immediately disable the HTTP/S server feature on all internet-facing Cisco IOS XE devices. This is the most effective immediate mitigation.
2. **Patch Deployment:** Implement the security fixes outlined in Cisco’s PSIRT advisory (https://sec.cloudapps.cisco.com/security/center/content/CiscoSecurityAdvisory/cisco-sa-iosxe-webui-privesc-j22SaA4z). Prioritize patching based on device criticality.
3. **Enhanced Monitoring:** Increase monitoring of affected devices for suspicious activity, including unusual user account creation, log deletions, and network traffic anomalies.
4. **Review Network Segmentation:**  Assess and implement network segmentation to limit the potential impact of a successful compromise.

**Next Steps:**

Our team is continuing to monitor the situation closely, track the threat actor's activities, and provide updates as new information becomes available. We will also be collaborating with Cisco and other security partners to share intelligence and coordinate defense efforts.
