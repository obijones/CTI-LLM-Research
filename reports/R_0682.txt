**Executive Threat Intelligence Report – Cisco IOS XE WebUI Privesc Vulnerabilities**

**To:** Senior Business Leadership
**From:** [Your Name/Cybersecurity Analysis Team]
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Urgent: Active Exploitation of Cisco IOS XE WebUI Vulnerabilities – Immediate Remediation Required

**Executive Summary:**

We are reporting an active and concerning security threat targeting Cisco IOS XE devices. A sophisticated actor is actively exploiting vulnerabilities (CVE-2023-20198 and CVE-2023-20273) within the WebUI interface to gain unauthorized administrative access. This activity has been ongoing since October 30th, 2023, following the release of proof-of-concept (POC) exploit code.  The threat actor is employing tactics including log clearing, user removal, and implant deployment, indicating a sustained and potentially long-term operational objective.  **Immediate action is required to mitigate this risk.**

**Key Findings & Analysis:**

* **Active Exploitation:**  Cisco Talos Intelligence has confirmed a significant increase in exploitation attempts following the publication of the POCs. This indicates widespread awareness and adoption of the exploit by malicious actors.
* **Threat Actor Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs):** The observed activity demonstrates a clear operational pattern:
    * **Reconnaissance:** The actor began with information gathering, utilizing commands to explore the device.
    * **Privilege Escalation:** Exploitation of the WebUI vulnerabilities directly led to unauthorized administrative access.
    * **Evidence Removal:**  The actor attempted to cover their tracks by clearing logs and removing user accounts (specifically "cisco_tac_admin” and “cisco_sys_manager”).
    * **Implant Deployment:**  The actor deployed a “BadCandy” web shell, suggesting a persistent access strategy.
* **Timeline of Events:** The initial suspicious activity was detected as early as September 18th, 2023, with the creation of the “cisco_tac_admin” account. Subsequent activity, including the deployment of the BadCandy shell, occurred in October.
* **Alignment with Guidance:** This activity aligns with recommendations from both Cisco and the U.S. government (CISA Directive 23-02) regarding the mitigation of risks associated with internet-exposed management interfaces.
* **Severity:**  These vulnerabilities are classified as critical, and the ongoing exploitation underscores the urgency of the situation.

**Immediate Recommendations:**

1. **Disable HTTP/S Server:**  Immediately disable the HTTP/S server feature on all internet-facing Cisco IOS XE devices as recommended by Cisco. This is the most effective immediate mitigation.
2. **Patch Deployment:**  Implement the security fixes outlined in Cisco’s PSIRT advisory (https://sec.cloudapps.cisco.com/security/center/content/CiscoSecurityAdvisory/cisco-sa-iosxe-webui-privesc-j22SaA4z) as quickly as possible.
3. **Network Monitoring:** Increase monitoring for suspicious activity, particularly related to user account creation, log deletion, and unusual command execution.
4. **Vulnerability Scanning:** Conduct thorough vulnerability scans of all affected devices to confirm patching and identify any remaining vulnerabilities.

**Conclusion:**

The exploitation of these Cisco IOS XE vulnerabilities represents a significant and active threat.  We urge immediate implementation of the recommended actions to minimize the potential impact. We will continue to monitor the situation closely and provide updated intelligence as it becomes available.
