**1. Executive Summary**

We’ve identified a significant escalation in the sophistication and global reach of business email compromise (BEC) scams. Recent activity, including a high-profile incident involving a Singapore-based commodity firm, demonstrates a clear trend: criminals are leveraging international payment systems and exploiting vulnerabilities in corporate communication channels. This report outlines the evolving threat landscape, potential business impacts, and recommended actions to proactively protect our organization.

**2. Potential Business Impact**

The impact of a successful BEC attack extends far beyond immediate financial losses. Beyond the direct theft of funds – as seen in the Singapore case – these attacks can severely damage our reputation, erode trust with clients and partners, disrupt operations, and trigger significant legal and regulatory scrutiny. The rapid movement of funds across borders, facilitated by systems like I-GRIP, amplifies the potential for widespread damage, demanding a swift and coordinated response. Furthermore, the time taken to identify and recover funds increases the likelihood of further losses and potential legal liabilities.

**3. Affected Systems / Scope**

The core vulnerability in this threat lies within the process of international payments and the reliance on external communication channels for verification. Specifically, the risk is present where employees are directed to make payments based on unsolicited requests received via email, particularly those mimicking legitimate supplier communications. The scope of this threat is global, impacting any organization that conducts international transactions or relies on email for critical business processes. The use of mechanisms like I-GRIP highlights the interconnectedness of the global financial ecosystem and the need for collaborative security efforts.

**4. Recommended Actions**

*   **Strengthen Employee Training:** Implement mandatory, ongoing training for all employees involved in financial transactions, focusing on recognizing and reporting suspicious emails and requests.
*   **Enhanced Payment Verification Protocols:** Establish stricter verification procedures for all international payments, including independent confirmation with the supplier via a separate, trusted communication channel.
*   **Collaborate with INTERPOL’s I-GRIP:** Immediately activate and maintain a strong working relationship with INTERPOL’s Financial Crime and Anti-Corruption Centre and leverage the I-GRIP mechanism for rapid detection and intervention.
*   **Continuous Threat Monitoring:** Increase investment in threat intelligence and monitoring capabilities to proactively identify and respond to emerging BEC tactics.
*   **Review Vendor Relationships:** Conduct a thorough review of our vendor relationships, particularly those involving international payments, to assess and mitigate potential vulnerabilities.
