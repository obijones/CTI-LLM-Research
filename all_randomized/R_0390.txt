**1. Executive Summary**

Threat actors are leveraging external Microsoft Teams collaboration to impersonate IT helpdesk staff and trick users into granting remote access, resulting in a significant risk to financial operations. This tactic, combined with sophisticated phishing campaigns, allows attackers to gain access to sensitive systems and potentially compromise financial data. Immediate action is required to contain the spread of this attack and prevent further disruption.

**2. Potential Business Impact**

A successful attack utilizing this method could lead to substantial operational disruption, potentially halting critical financial processes and impacting revenue streams. Furthermore, unauthorized access to systems could expose sensitive financial data, resulting in significant financial losses due to fraud, regulatory fines (related to data protection regulations), and damage to the organization’s reputation.

**3. Affected Systems / Scope**

This attack targets the ability of users to securely access systems and data, directly impacting key operational functions within the organization. Specifically, the compromised access allows attackers to potentially manipulate financial transactions, access confidential customer information, and disrupt critical business workflows. The attack’s rapid spread highlights the vulnerability of relying on trusted vendor relationships for access control.

**4. Recommended Actions**

*   **Immediately Revoke Session Cookies:** Direct IT security teams to immediately revoke all active session cookies for all user accounts, effectively terminating any unauthorized remote access sessions initiated by the threat actor. This is a critical step to prevent further access to compromised systems.
*   **Roll Back Recent MFA Modifications:** Direct IT security teams to roll back any recent modifications made to Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) configurations for affected user accounts. This action will remove any vulnerabilities introduced by the attacker’s manipulation of security settings.
*   **Initiate Rapid Response with Microsoft 365 Defender:** Direct Microsoft 365 Defender to contain the attack, including monitoring for additional compromised users and changes in phishing patterns.
*   **Conduct Thorough Account Audits:** Direct the organization’s security team to conduct a comprehensive audit of all user accounts, focusing on those with elevated privileges or access to sensitive financial systems. This will identify any additional compromised accounts and strengthen overall security posture.
*   **Enhance Threat Hunting Capabilities:** Direct the organization to prioritize proactive threat hunting activities, specifically targeting anomalous email behavior and user activity patterns, to identify and remediate similar threats before they can cause significant damage.
