**Executive Threat Intelligence Report: Cisco IOS XE WebUI Privesc Vulnerabilities – October 2023 Activity**

**To:** Senior Business Leadership
**From:** [Your Name/Cybersecurity Analysis Team]
**Date:** November 3, 2023
**Subject:** Urgent: Active Exploitation of Cisco IOS XE WebUI Vulnerabilities – Immediate Mitigation Required

**Executive Summary:**

This report details an ongoing and active exploitation campaign targeting Cisco IOS XE devices utilizing a privilege escalation vulnerability (CVE-2023-20198 and CVE-2023-20273).  Threat actors are actively leveraging proof-of-concept (POC) exploits, demonstrating significant malicious intent and posing a critical risk to our network infrastructure.  We’ve observed a clear pattern of reconnaissance, evidence clearing, and implant deployment, indicating a sophisticated and persistent attack. Immediate action is required to mitigate this threat.

**Key Findings:**

* **Active Exploitation:**  Following the publication of POCs on October 30th and 31st, we’ve witnessed a dramatic increase in exploitation attempts. This confirms the widespread availability and adoption of the exploit by malicious actors.
* **Threat Actor Tactics:** The threat actor is employing established tactics, including:
    * **Reconnaissance:** Gathering information about devices using commands like `clear logging` and user removal.
    * **Evidence Clearing:**  Deleting logs and removing user accounts (specifically `cisco_tac_admin`, `cisco_sys_manager`, and `cisco_support`) to obscure their activity.
    * **Implant Deployment:** Establishing persistent access via the deployment of a “BadCandy” web shell (a Lua-based web shell) – indicating a long-term presence on compromised systems.
* **Timeline of Activity:** Initial suspicious activity was detected as early as September 18th, with a significant cluster of activity observed in October, coinciding with the release of the POCs.  This suggests a deliberate and phased approach to compromise.
* **Cisco Response:** Cisco’s PSIRT team is actively tracking the exploitation and has released a security advisory (Cisco-SA-iosxe-webui-privesc-j22SaA4z) with immediate remediation steps.  Support centers collaborated with security to correlate indicators.
* **Guidance Alignment:** This activity aligns with recommendations from both Cisco and the U.S. government (CISA Directive 23-02) regarding the mitigation of risk associated with internet-exposed management interfaces.

**Severity Assessment:**

* **Critical:** The vulnerabilities allow for unauthenticated remote privilege escalation, granting attackers full administrative control over affected devices.
* **High:** The ongoing exploitation and observed tactics demonstrate a significant and immediate threat.

**Recommendations (Immediate Action Required):**

1. **Disable HTTP/S Server:** Immediately disable the HTTP/S server feature on all internet-facing Cisco IOS XE devices, as recommended by Cisco.
2. **Patch Deployment:** Prioritize the deployment of the security patches outlined in the Cisco PSIRT advisory.
3. **Network Monitoring:** Enhance network monitoring to detect suspicious activity, including unusual user account creation, log deletions, and command execution.
4. **Vulnerability Scanning:** Conduct thorough vulnerability scans to identify any remaining vulnerable devices.

**Next Steps:**

We are continuing to monitor the threat landscape and will provide further updates as new information becomes available.  We will also be conducting a full post-incident review to identify areas for improvement in our security posture.
