**Executive Report: Advanced Phishing Campaign Targeting Financial Services**

**1. Executive Summary**

A sophisticated adversary, designated Storm-1167, is conducting a multi-stage phishing and business email compromise (BEC) attack targeting banking and financial services organizations. This campaign leverages trusted vendor relationships to gain access and escalate attacks, posing a significant risk to our organization’s operations and financial stability. Immediate action is required to mitigate the threat and prevent potential financial loss and reputational damage.

**2. Potential Business Impact**

This attack represents a serious threat to our business, potentially leading to significant operational disruption. The campaign’s use of indirect proxy and sophisticated techniques to evade defenses could compromise sensitive financial data, resulting in substantial financial losses due to fraudulent transactions and recovery costs. Furthermore, a successful attack could severely damage our reputation, eroding customer trust and impacting our market position.

**3. Affected Systems / Scope**

The attack demonstrates the vulnerability of organizations relying on external vendor relationships. Specifically, the campaign’s initial entry point involved a compromised trusted vendor, highlighting the risk associated with supply chain security. This exposes our organization’s operations to disruption if we are connected to a compromised vendor, potentially impacting critical business processes such as payments, customer service, and regulatory reporting.

**4. Recommended Actions**

*   **Immediately Revoke Session Cookies:** Direct the IT security team to immediately revoke the compromised user’s session cookie, as described in the incident response. This action directly addresses the second-stage BEC activity initiated by the attacker, preventing further unauthorized access and potential data exfiltration.
*   **Implement Continuous Access Evaluation:** Direct the IT security team to implement continuous access evaluation. This proactive measure will continuously assess user access rights, identifying and restricting access for users exhibiting suspicious behavior, thereby reducing the risk of unauthorized access and potential compromise.
*   **Invest in Advanced Anti-Phishing Solutions:** Direct the IT security team to invest in advanced anti-phishing solutions that monitor and scan incoming emails and visited websites. This will proactively block malicious websites, including those used in this phishing campaign, and solutions that detect and block malicious emails, links, and files.
*   **Continuously Monitor Suspicious or Anomalous Activities:** Direct the IT security team to continuously monitor suspicious or anomalous activities. Hunt for sign-in attempts with suspicious characteristics (for example, location, ISP, user agent, and use of anonymizer services). This ongoing vigilance will enable rapid detection and response to evolving attack tactics.
*   **Initiate a Rapid Response with Microsoft Defender Experts:** Direct the IT security team to initiate rapid response with Microsoft Defender Experts to contain the attack, including monitoring the attack for any additional compromised users or changes in the phishing patterns as it rapidly unfolded into a large-scale campaign.
