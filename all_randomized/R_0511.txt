**Executive Threat Intelligence Report – Cisco IOS XE WebUI Privesc Vulnerabilities**

**To:** Senior Business Leadership
**From:** [Your Name/Cybersecurity Analysis Team]
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Urgent: Active Exploitation of Cisco IOS XE WebUI Vulnerabilities – Immediate Mitigation Required

**Executive Summary:**

We are reporting an active and escalating threat targeting Cisco IOS XE devices due to previously disclosed vulnerabilities (CVE-2023-20198 and CVE-2023-20273). Threat actors are actively exploiting these vulnerabilities, deploying a malicious web shell (“BadCandy”) to gain administrative control, and attempting to cover their tracks.  This represents a significant risk to our organization’s network infrastructure and requires immediate action.

**Key Findings:**

* **Active Exploitation:** Proof-of-concept (POC) code for these vulnerabilities was released on October 30 and 31, 2023, triggering a rapid increase in exploitation attempts.
* **Threat Actor Tactics:** The threat actor is employing established tactics, including reconnaissance, log clearing, and user account manipulation (creating “cisco_tac_admin” and “cisco_sys_manager” accounts). This indicates a sophisticated and persistent attacker.
* **Implant Deployment:** Evidence suggests the attacker is deploying a “BadCandy” web shell, likely to establish persistent access and potentially deploy further malicious payloads.
* **Timeline of Activity:** Suspicious activity began as early as September 18th, 2023, with the creation of the “cisco_tac_admin” account from a suspicious IP address.  Further activity, including evidence of the BadCandy deployment, was observed in October.
* **Cisco Response:** Cisco’s PSIRT team is actively tracking the exploitation and has released a security advisory (https://sec.cloudapps.cisco.com/security/center/content/CiscoSecurityAdvisory/cisco-sa-iosxe-webui-privesc-j22SaA4z) with remediation steps.
* **Alignment with Guidance:** This activity aligns with broader security recommendations from the U.S. government (Binding Operational Directive 23-02) and Cisco’s collaboration with industry partners through the Network Resilience Coalition.

**Risk Assessment:**

* **Severity:** High – These vulnerabilities allow for complete system compromise, potentially leading to data breaches, service disruption, and further attacks.
* **Impact:** Significant – Compromised devices could be used as entry points to our network, impacting critical systems and data.
* **Likelihood:** Currently High – The availability of POCs and observed exploitation attempts indicate a high likelihood of continued attacks.

**Recommendations (Immediate Action Required):**

1. **Disable HTTP/S Server:** Immediately disable the HTTP/S server feature on all internet-facing Cisco IOS XE devices, as recommended by Cisco. This is the most effective immediate mitigation.
2. **Patching:** Prioritize patching affected devices with the latest security updates released by Cisco.
3. **Network Monitoring:** Increase network monitoring for suspicious activity, particularly related to command execution and user account creation.
4. **Vulnerability Scanning:** Conduct thorough vulnerability scans of all affected devices.
5. **Review Security Posture:**  Assess and strengthen overall network security controls.

**Next Steps:**

Our team will continue to monitor the situation closely, provide updates, and assist with implementation of the recommended mitigation steps. We will also be collaborating with Cisco’s PSIRT team to stay informed of any new developments.
